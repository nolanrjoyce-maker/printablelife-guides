How to Write Pet Medication Instructions for a Sitter

What to write down so a sitter gives the right dose at the right time, and what to do if a dose is missed or refused.

Write one line per medication

  • Name exactly as it appears on the label, the strength (for example 5 mg), and the dose (for example one tablet).
  • When: clock times rather than 'twice a day', so doses stay evenly spaced.
  • With or without food, and what to hide it in if your pet refuses plain pills.
  • Where it is stored, including anything that must be refrigerated.

Plan for the problems

  • Ask your vet now what to do if a dose is missed, vomited or spat out, and write the answer down word for word.
  • Note side effects your vet told you to watch for and when they warrant a call.
  • Pre-portion doses into labeled bags or a weekly pill organizer so the sitter can see at a glance what's been given.

Use a sign-off log

  • A simple table with date, time, medication, dose and the sitter's initials prevents double-dosing.
  • Ask the sitter to text a photo of the log each day.

Before you leave

  • Refill so you have enough for the trip plus a few extra days.
  • Leave the pharmacy's phone number and the prescription number.
  • Give the first dose together with your sitter during a trial visit.
