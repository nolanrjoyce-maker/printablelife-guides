How to Keep Car Maintenance Records (and Why They Matter at Resale)

What to record for every oil change, repair and inspection, how long to keep receipts, and how organized service history helps when you sell.

Why records matter

  • Buyers and dealers often ask for service history; a complete record is easier to trust than a verbal account.
  • Many manufacturer warranties require proof that scheduled maintenance was done.
  • A running log shows what's due next, so you don't repeat or skip a service.

What to write down every time

  • Date and odometer reading.
  • What was done (for example: oil and filter, tire rotation, brake pads) and the parts or fluids used.
  • Who did it: shop name, or yourself.
  • Cost, and where the receipt is filed.

What to keep

  • Receipts and invoices for maintenance and repairs, in date order.
  • Inspection and emissions certificates, recall work and warranty repairs.
  • Tire purchase and alignment records.

Presenting history when you sell

  • Put a one-page summary on top: mileage, major services, and any open issues you know about.
  • Order receipts by date behind it so a buyer can check any line.
  • Be accurate about gaps; an honest record is more persuasive than a padded one.
